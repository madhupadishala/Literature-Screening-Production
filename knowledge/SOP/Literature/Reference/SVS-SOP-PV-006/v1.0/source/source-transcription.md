## 2. Definitions

### Literature Search

A pharmacovigilance literature search systematically searches and reviews published literature, medical journals, scientific articles, conference proceedings, regulatory databases and other credible sources to identify and evaluate adverse drug reactions and other safety concerns associated with medicinal products.

Its objectives include:
- identifying new or previously unknown adverse reactions;
- assessing frequency and severity of known adverse reactions;
- evaluating drug-drug interactions and contraindications;
- monitoring emerging safety concerns;
- providing evidence for regulatory decisions;
- supporting patient safety and rational use of medicines.

Searches use product-specific keywords, MeSH terms and tailored search strategies. Retrieved information is analysed and relevant findings are reported through the appropriate PV/regulatory process.

### Special situations

**Lack of efficacy:** unexpected failure of a medicinal product to produce the intended effect based on previous scientific investigation.

**Off-label use:** intentional use for an indication or population not covered by the approved labelling.

**Overdose:** administration of a medicinal product above the maximum recommended dose per administration or per day, including cumulative overdose.

**Misuse:** intentional and inappropriate use not in accordance with prescribed/authorised dose, route, indication or legal supply status.

**Abuse:** sporadic or persistent intentional excessive use accompanied by harmful physical or psychological effects.

**Medication error:** unintentional error in prescribing, dispensing or administration while the product is under the control of a healthcare professional, patient or consumer.

**Occupational exposure:** exposure to a medicinal product for human use as a result of occupation.

## 3. Objective

Describe the literature-search procedure for reportable adverse events and further safety-relevant information, and screen for new safety signals and systematic literature for individual medicinal products owned by the MAH.

## 4. Scope

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Literature Search Set-up

The Project Manager/Designee collaborates with the MAH to define search scope, content, sources (e.g. PubMed, Medline, Embase), range, frequency and approved search terms. Requirements are communicated to the LMA and documented in the project repository.

Literature search and review may be conducted using an AI-based tool or MAH-specific tool depending on customer preferences.

Where feasible, the LMA performs a preliminary test of search terms before the standard search.

The LMA coordinates local-journal searches in countries where the product is marketed and arranges English translation when needed.

Additional products to be added to literature search should be communicated by the MAH in advance as specified in the SoW.

Generic search strategies may combine brand/product/active-ingredient terms with safety concepts including, among others:
- adverse drug reaction;
- overdose;
- misuse;
- abuse/substance abuse;
- pregnancy;
- efficacy/lack of efficacy;
- drug withdrawal/tolerance;
- medication error;
- death/fatality;
- drug interaction;
- carcinogenicity;
- off-label use;
- occupational exposure;
- toxicity/intoxication;
- contraindication;
- congenital disorder;
- treatment failure;
- lactation;
- case report;
- environmental exposure;
- addiction/dependence;
- poisoning;
- refractory/resistant;
- injury;
- suicide;
- pregnancy-related terms;
- damage.

### 6.2 Comprehensive Global Search

The LMA accesses agreed literature databases owned by SafeVig or the MAH.

The search is executed across all agreed databases and uses drug name/brand, active moiety/salt forms and the approved date range.

Search output is labelled with drug name and covered date interval.

The LMA validates retrieved output for ICSR accuracy. A QR/MR performs peer review. Valid reports proceed to the ICSR team. Questions may be escalated to a PV physician, and communications are documented.

Weekly search output is recorded in the Literature Output and Validation Tracker (SVS-SOP-PV-006-TMP-001).

### 6.3 Local Literature Search

Journal selection is coordinated with the local PV contact and MAH, focusing on relevant medical/scientific journals in the territory.

Local-journal lists are cross-referenced with global databases and EMA Medical Literature Monitoring to avoid duplicate cases.

Approved journal lists and SoW search terms are provided to the Local PV Contact.

Frequency follows journal availability (for example weekly or monthly).

The local search is performed according to the SoW. Safety-relevant results are documented in the Documentation of Literature Search form and reviewed by QR/MR. Safety-relevant information and required actions are communicated to the Project Manager/Designee and MAH.

### 6.4 Full Text Article and Translation Requests

If a full-text article is not publicly available, it is ordered in agreement with the MAH using SVS-SOP-PV-006-TMP-002.

On receipt, the FTA is forwarded to QR/MR.

Translation responsibilities are defined in the SoW/contract. Summary information may be translated first; complete FTA translation is then provided to QR/MR, LMA, Project Manager/Designee and Client/Sponsor according to agreed timelines.

### 6.5 Assessment of Day 0

Day Zero is the date the organisation becomes aware of a publication containing the minimum information required for an ICSR to meet submission criteria. Awareness includes employees/MAH personnel and contracted third parties.

For literature reports, the SOP states Day Zero corresponds to the date the literature output is generated from the literature database.

### 6.6 Periodic Quality Review

Personnel performing literature quality checks receive specialised PV/literature/database training.

Literature output undergoes quality review. Validated reports proceed to final validation and case creation according to SVS-SOP-PV-001 ICSR Handling and Processing in Post-Marketing or the MAH/Client/Sponsor procedure.

### 6.7 Translated Documents / Retrospective Search

Weekly retrospective literature searches are performed.

Full-text articles containing valid cases or safety-related information are obtained from free full-text sites, libraries, purchase, or MAH sources.

Foreign-language articles are translated through an official partner/vendor according to MAH requirements. Translation requests are initiated promptly and governed by contractual timelines.

The process links to SVS-WI-PV-002 Triage in Case Management.

### 6.8 Aggregate and Signal Support

At client request, Aggregate and Signal teams request literature outputs, review them and provide them to the client for final confirmation. Full texts or translations are obtained for confirmed reports.

### 6.9 EudraVigilance Medical Literature Monitoring

Where contracted, SafeVig accesses/downloads MLM ICSRs from EudraVigilance.

The MLM record includes, as applicable:
- substance group;
- reference databases;
- search date/time;
- article title/authors/journal/DOI or database reference;
- primary-author country;
- screening date;
- inclusion criteria for further case processing/ICSR creation;
- potential/confirmed ICSR flag;
- serious/non-serious adverse-reaction flag;
- primary-source country;
- full-text/translation request and receipt dates;
- medicinal products/substances identified as suspect/interacting;
- worldwide unique case identifier;
- ICSR creation/transmission date.

Before ICSR creation, duplicate checks are performed for cases from the same article and later against other literature/non-literature sources.

Articles not meeting valid ICSR criteria are archived in an exclusion group with the applicable exclusion criteria. Articles containing one or more valid ICSRs are archived in an inclusion group with inclusion criteria. Full-text screening outcome and potential ICSR processing are documented in the safety database.

For suspected serious adverse reactions, risk-based follow-up with the primary author may be performed when outcome or important clinical information is missing, or minimum criteria are incomplete.

### 6.10 Search Results to MAH

The LMA shares the validation tracker with the MAH at agreed intervals, including weekly/monthly reports as applicable.

### 6.11 Handling of Safety-Relevant Cases

If case processing is in scope, identified cases are entered into the safety database under SOP-PV-001.

If case processing is not in scope, the LMA sends the literature-search summary, relevant publications and case extracts to the MAH.

### 6.12 Filing and Archiving

Essential literature-search documents and forms are filed according to the agreed electronic/paper filing system and the applicable archiving SOP.

### 6.13 PSUR and Signal Literature Search

Literature searches supporting PSUR/Signal activities are performed after the applicable data-lock point. The Aggregate team provides data in the predefined format and the literature team reviews it.

Personnel undergo training, shadowing and performance evaluation before independent production work.

The source document contains a Literature Search flow-chart covering setup, global/local search, search-result documentation, quality review, medical review, requests for full publication/translation and downstream case-processing paths.
